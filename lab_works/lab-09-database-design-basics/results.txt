============================================================
1. First Normal Form (1NF)
============================================================

Output:

```text
CREATE TABLE
INSERT 0 1
```

SELECT * FROM students_bad;

```text
 student_id |     name      | phone_numbers
------------+---------------+----------------
          1 | Anna Ivanova  | 111-111, 222-222
(1 row)
```

SELECT * FROM students_lab9;

```text
 student_id |     name
------------+---------------
          1 | Anna Ivanova
(1 row)
```

SELECT * FROM students_phones;

```text
 student_id | phone_number | phone_type
------------+--------------+-----------
          1 | 111-111      | Mobile
          1 | 222-222      | Work
(2 rows)
```

The unnormalized table stores multiple phone numbers in one column. 
The normalized design stores each phone number in a separate row.

2. Second Normal Form (2NF)
============================================================

SELECT * FROM enrollments_bad;

```text
 student_id | course_id | course_name      | grade
------------+-----------+------------------+------
          1 |       101 | Database Systems | A
          2 |       101 | Database Systems | B+
(2 rows)
```

SELECT * FROM courses_lab9;

```text
 course_id | course_name      | credits
-----------+------------------+--------
       101 | Database Systems |       3
(1 row)
```

SELECT * FROM enrollments_lab9;

```text
 student_id | course_id | grade
------------+-----------+------
          1 |       101 | A
          2 |       101 | B+
(2 rows)
```

Course information is stored separately from enrollment records, reducing repeated course details.

============================================================
3. Third Normal Form (3NF)
============================================================

SELECT * FROM students_bad_3nf;

```text
 student_id |     name     | department_id | department_name
------------+--------------+---------------+----------------
          1 | Anna Ivanova |            10 | Computer Science
          2 | Peter Smith  |            10 | Computer Science
(2 rows)
```

SELECT * FROM departments_lab9;

```text
 department_id | department_name | department_head
---------------+-----------------+----------------
            10 | Computer Science | Alice Johnson
(1 row)
```

SELECT * FROM students_3nf_lab9;

```text
 student_id |     name     | department_id
------------+--------------+---------------
          1 | Anna Ivanova |            10
          2 | Peter Smith  |            10
(2 rows)
```

JOIN query result:

```text
 student_id |     name     | department_name | department_head
------------+--------------+-----------------+----------------
          1 | Anna Ivanova | Computer Science | Alice Johnson
          2 | Peter Smith  | Computer Science | Alice Johnson
(2 rows)
```

Department information is stored in a separate table and referenced by department_id.

4. Library Database Design
============================================================

```text
-----------+------------+-----------+------------
         1 | George     | Orwell    | 1903-06-25
         2 | Jane       | Austen    | 1775-12-16
```

### Books


============================================================
Conclusion
============================================================

- 1NF: Phone numbers are stored in separate rows.
- 2NF: Course details are separated from enrollment records.
- 3NF: Department information is stored in a separate table.
- The library database uses primary keys, foreign keys, and a junction table for the many-to-many relationship between books and authors.
- JOIN queries retrieve related information from multiple tables.
